AUM Apparel

A sustainable activewear brand from Bali with a real story and no way to tell it in eight seconds. We built the account around the story instead of around the discount.

3.92xROAS over eight months
756Purchases
Rp359.235Average order value, +40%

The challenge

AUM Apparel makes activewear shaped by Balinese craft and sustainability, and was competing against international brands with a fraction of the media budget. The value was real; the advertising was not carrying it.

  • Average cost per click sat at Rp8.043, high for the category.
  • Audiences were broad, so budget reached people with no interest in craft or sustainability.
  • Creative showed product on white, which made a story-led brand look like a commodity.

What we changed

1. Targeting built on values, not demographics

We combined sustainable fashion buyers aged 25 to 45 with people who engage with Balinese culture and responsible tourism, then built lookalikes from customers whose orders mentioned Bali or sustainable in any field. Smaller audiences, far higher intent.

2. Creative that shows where the product comes from

The core film ran fifteen seconds: local craftspeople, natural materials, the island itself. Product shots stayed, but they were no longer the first frame. The line "made in Bali, offered to the world" carried every placement.

3. Spend concentrated on what already worked

Cost per purchase was capped at Rp100.000, well below the Rp359.235 order value. Budget moved only to campaigns holding above 3x ROAS, which meant fewer campaigns running and more money behind each.

Results

Eight months, 28 April to 31 December 2025, with Rp69,3 million in media spend.

Rp271,5 jtRevenue generated
3.92xROAS, industry 1.5–2.5x
Rp91.738Cost per purchase

Average order value rose 40 percent to Rp359.235, and cost per purchase landed 75 percent below it. The account now runs on fewer, better-funded campaigns rather than a wide spread of tests.

What we would do next

  • Build a retention flow, since repeat buyers are the cheapest revenue in apparel.
  • Test TikTok at 10 percent of spend, judged against the same cost-per-purchase bar.
  • Turn the craftspeople film into a series, one story per collection.
